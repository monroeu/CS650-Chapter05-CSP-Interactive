# CS650 Chapter 5 — Instructor Quick Guide

**Audience:** students encountering CSPs for the first time.

1. Begin with the variables, possible values (domains), and legal combinations (constraints).
2. Ask students to describe what the diagram represents in ordinary language.
3. Advance one step. Have students identify every eliminated domain value.
4. Read the *Observe*, *Explain why*, and *Predict* cards. Ask students to justify each answer.
5. Ask whether the algorithm is *assigning a value*, *eliminating candidates*, or *reversing a choice*.
6. Use **Previous step** to revisit a mistake. At a lesson boundary it returns to the last step of the preceding lesson.
7. Use **Previous subsection** to restart a preceding subsection from its beginning.
8. Use **Section overview** to go to the first subsection in the section.
9. Complete the knowledge check and transfer challenge at the end of each subsection.
10. Contrast search and propagation; a reduced domain does not always mean the variable has been assigned.

## Example teaching prompts by section

- **5.1:** Identify what is a variable, a value, and a constraint in a university schedule.
- **5.2:** Explain why an AC-3 candidate lacks support and why a changed domain causes a neighboring arc to be revisited.
- **5.3:** Compare a choice that fails immediately with a choice that causes a contradiction several levels later.
- **5.4:** Count conflicts before and after a queen's move, then explain a local minimum.
- **5.5:** Identify disconnected components and why solving a tree avoids general cyclic search.
